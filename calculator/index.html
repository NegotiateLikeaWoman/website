Three steps to start closing it

  1. Is there a path to closing the gap where you are?

    In a challenging job market that favours employers, it can be very difficult to close a significant, longstanding pay gap without a change in the scope of your role. The existing arrangement has become a precedent, even if the gap is acknowledged.

    A substantial shift may require a step up, an expansion of responsibilities or a shift towards higher-value activities. That gives you a credible argument that your value proposition to the firm has changed—and that your compensation should change with it.

    Is that realistic where you are? It depends on the firm's size, its situation and priorities, the opportunities available, and your relationship with the people whose support you would need.

  2. Where could you make a uniquely valuable contribution?

    What problem or opportunity matters most to your line manager? What evidence can you present that you are uniquely well positioned to deliver it—or a key component of it? What change in your role would allow you to deliver that?

    A proposition or an ask that makes the boss's life easier or allows them to sleep at night is far easier to say yes to.

  3. Whose backing would make it happen?

    Your line manager may not be the only person who influences your role and compensation. Whose opinions do they trust? Who is their sounding board? Who might have the power of veto? And who would you want advocating for you?

    Once you know who those people are, repeat the same process: what matters to them? How could you help them achieve it? How can you align your interests so you can help each other?

    Start ahead of time. The longer you put off those conversations, the more likely it is that someone else's name is already in the picture—or that they have been lined up for the opportunity.
