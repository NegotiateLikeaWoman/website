What it is

Changing what happens in the conversations that shape your career.

Pay, promotion, authority, opportunity — the negotiations that shape what you're rewarded for, what you're trusted with and what happens next.

Most negotiation training ends the week you finish it, which is almost never the week your negotiation happens.

Two months of method. Ten months of doing it for real.

Because leverage takes months to build, and the timing is something you can influence but rarely control.

Why this is different

You're not bad at negotiation — you're playing a harder game.

Research consistently finds it is riskier for women to act assertively in negotiation. Behaviour that's perfectly acceptable coming from a man triggers backlash when it comes from a woman.

Which means the hesitation you may have been treating as a personal weakness is closer to an accurate reading of the room. Holding back is a rational response to a genuine risk. It's simply a very expensive one — and it isn't the only response available.

Most negotiation training ignores this. It teaches the tactics without accounting for the fact that the same behaviour can land differently coming from a woman.

Negotiating like a woman doesn't mean negotiating softly. It means seeing the game you're actually in — one where the rules aren't applied evenly — and choosing your strategy with that in full view. Sometimes that means being direct, anchoring hard, and letting a silence sit. Sometimes it means framing an ask so that saying yes is easy for the person who has to defend it upstairs.

Often, there's far more you can unlock where you are. Sometimes, though, the honest answer is that you've reached the ceiling of what your current environment will offer you. Knowing which situation you're in changes the strategy.

The cost of being undervalued

What could that gap add up to?

Think of a negotiation earlier in your career — one where you might have secured more, or didn't realise there was anything to secure. At the time, the difference probably looked modest.

But a lower starting point can follow you. When later rises build on it, the gap compounds.

And that's one conversation.

Now think about a whole career — every review, offer, promotion and opportunity where the outcome could have been different.

And that's only the money. Negotiating well can also change the authority you have, the opportunities you're offered, the time you keep for yourself and the decisions you get to influence. Those are harder to put a number on.

Who it's for

A small group, at a similar point.

You've been doing work that's visibly above your grade, and it hasn't translated into anything. You've been told "next year" more than once. You've watched someone less capable be promoted, and been asked to be pleased about it.

You're respected. You're also underpaid, and everyone including you knows it. Or the money is fine and what you actually want is more authority, autonomy or a role that leaves room for a life outside work.

It's about the size of the gap between what you contribute and what comes back — and whether you're ready to do something deliberate about it.

It isn't for you if you'd rather watch than take part, if you want someone to do it for you, or if you'd rather not look at your own part in it.

What the twelve months look like

Two months of Foundations. Then ten months of doing it for real.

Eight weekly 90-minute sessions, October to December, built around the CLOSE framework.

C — Clarify

What you want next, where the opportunity lies and what needs to change.

L — Leverage

The value you create, how others perceive it and who advocates for you.

O — Options

Parameters, fallbacks, anchors and widening what's on the table.

S — Steer

How to lead the negotiation rather than be led by it.

E — Embody

The mindset and self-regulation to make the ask.

Review & Reflect

Learn from every negotiation and carry it into the next.

Two live practice sessions in pairs, followed by group review.

Then ten months of applying it. Monthly group surgeries where you bring what's live. Three guest experts through the year, including Emilie Cousteau on executive presence. A private WhatsApp group for what can't wait. Three cohort dinners in London, included and entirely optional. And 1:1 time with me whenever something significant is in play.
